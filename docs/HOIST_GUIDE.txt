1. THE GAME IN A MINUTE
-----------------------
You sail the Merry Gull, a small ship, with your first mate Mara. The captain (you) never speaks.
It plays as a cosy Caribbean sailing game: sail, fish, dig, deliver, chat, trade.
The further you sail from home (Tama), the more the world goes wrong. That is "dread": it follows where you
are, never the clock. Nothing is ever explained.
In a black hut a silent figure throws a twenty-sided die and writes the numbers down. You throw too.
What you have understood by the end decides what you can do at the Table by the Water, where every ending happens.

The demo build is a different game: it ends at the first throw at the hut, and has no dread at all.


2. CONTROLS
-----------
Keyboard (every key except Esc, P, Enter, arrows, digits and F-keys can now be remapped:
Pause > Settings > Keys; prompts on screen follow your keys).

  A / D, left / right    steer
  W / S, up / down       let the sails out / reef them (with oars out: row / back water)
  G                      oars out / in (dead calm, or creeping up to a pier)
  E                      the one "do" key: go ashore, dig, pick fruit, salvage, hail, patch a sail...
                         Works under way: the anchor drops by itself and the action starts.
  C                      fish (also under way: the anchor drops first)
  Space / Enter          move Mara's line on; Space fires the cannons when a raider is engaged
  L                      lamp on / off
  M / J                  the captain's log (M opens the chart, J the orders)
  Q                      show / hide the tracker slip
  Y                      ask Mara for a hint
  R / F, mouse drag      tilt the camera; Z / X / V look left / right / ahead
  - / =, mouse wheel     zoom
  H                      hide the HUD
  Esc / P                pause

Controller (new: every screen now works by pad)
  Left stick     steer (analogue now) and sails;  in menus: move the focus
  D-pad          arrows; in menus: move the focus
  A              E (interact); in menus: press the focused button
  B              back one level, then close (does nothing at sea)
  X              fish (C)
  Y              the log
  RT             Space (fire, throw, go on)
  LT             oars (G)
  LB / RB        look around; in menus: change tab or bookmark
  Right stick    look around (sideways) and zoom (up / down)
  View           tap: tracker; hold 0.6 s: hint
  Start          pause
  Left stick in  lamp
Rumble is optional (Pause > Settings > Display), on for bites, catches, hits and the anchor biting; never for anything frightening.


3. HOW TO PLAY: THE FIRST HOUR
------------------------------
The main story runs in chapters. Mara nudges you; nothing is marked on the map except the first few goals.

  Chapter I    Find your sea legs; make landfall at Tama.
  Chapter II   Follow Perrin's riddle (dig for treasure); spend your loot at the shipwright.
  Chapter III  Dead calm (row); beat to windward; earn your keep (a delivery); take a passenger; chart the sea.
  Chapter IV   Meet the locals (tavern); a note from Perrin; check his chart; his second riddle.
  Chapter V    What the sea says (bottles); ask around (gossip).
  Chapter VI   Find the hut. The first throw. A rusted diver stands on the water. (The demo ends here.)
  After        Perrin's five letters send you into each region in turn (Crown, Free Cays, Lantern, Reef, Drowned).

Until the first throw a fog wall holds you in the chapter's waters, however well fitted your ship is
(this was tightened this round: before, a well-upgraded ship could slip out early). After the throw the sea opens;
only your ship (hull, sails, lantern) and what you have learned take you further out.

Side goals (optional): fish, hail a ship, pick up a bottle, buy paint, pick fruit, see dolphins, odd jobs from a
harbour board, salvage a wreck, sink a raider.

Tips that are now true:
  - You never have to stop the ship by hand. Press E or C under way; the anchor does the rest.
  - Pausing or opening the log no longer ends a dig or a fishing fight. They wait for you.
  - Hold E for under a second on a dig beach to pack up and leave (it counts as a failed dig).
  - Selling at a harbour keeps back what an errand in hand needs.
  - Docking lays the ship alongside at rest.


4. THE SHIP AND ITS SYSTEMS
---------------------------
Sailing: the wind wanders. Running before it is fast; you cannot sail straight into it, so you tack.
  Gusts show as dark patches on the water; current lines as foam streaks. The wind ring on the HUD shows the wind.
  The wind no longer announces its shifts in words, and crossing into a region no longer shows a banner:
  the wind ring and the place line show both.
Oars (G): slow (2.6 a second) but work in a dead calm and against everything.
Interacting (E): one target at a time, picked in a fixed order. New forgiving rules:
  the last thing in reach stays usable for 0.6 s; an E pressed a quarter-second early still counts;
  a running action keeps its target even if a boat drifts past.
Fishing (C): cast, wait for the bite, strike on the mark (60 ms of grace either side; never on a red snag),
  then hold Space to keep the marker in the green. 11 species by place and hour; dark ones with dread and at night.
  The Catch book in the Ship's book has a pixel portrait of each.
Digging (E on a riddle beach): an 8 x 5 beach; read the riddle; the noon shadow can point.
Deliveries, passengers, commissions: from harbour boards. Crown deliveries have deadlines.
Upgrades (7, three levels each): sails, rudder, lantern, spyglass, hull, cannons, crew.
  Lantern 3 now really does keep the dark back: the lamp burns its oil at 0.8 of lantern 2.
Lamp oil: the lamp burns oil at night; out in the open dark it drains fast.
Combat (optional): only some Reef ships are raiders. New: 0.7 s before a raider fires you see a puff at her gun side
  and she holds her turn. Under half her hull she lists and smokes; under a quarter she runs.
  While a raider is engaged, Space only fires (it no longer skips Mara's line instead).
The log: three bookmarks only (Chart, Orders, Ship's book). The chart is an inked portolan: painted means sailed.
The HUD stays wordless: gold, time, place, the slip.


5. THE REGIONS AND HOW EACH PLAYS
---------------------------------
Home Sound (Tama)  Bright, gentle, the bells ring softly. No dread. New: the noon slack, the fishers' marks,
                   gulls that fly home at dusk, a Sunday race.
Crown Traders (north)  Deadlines and a shared record of arrivals (ticks and black lines) at every Crown harbour;
                   customs cutters; buoyed lanes (coming in between the buoys is a clean arrival).
                   On time pays 1.45x, late 0.55x; up to 15 s late now still counts.
                   New: pennants on the destination's mast count down your hours.
Free Cays (east)   Raiders only by day; the night market pays 1.4x; dusk fish. The Two Players keep the Cays'
                   nights quiet. New: the harbours sleep by day (07:00 to 17:00).
Reef Brotherhood (south-west)  Wrecks pay 1.5x; bounties for sunk raiders; storms bring wrecks up.
                   The coral heads are off for now (reefs will come back as a fixed design).
Lantern Guild (west)  Fish dear; the Guild buys lamp oil; half the lighthouses are dark; relight one with 30 oil
                   for pay and the local chart. New: every light flashes its own rhythm; a keeper trades oil for fish.
The Drowned (far out, full game only)  The shoal belt is off for now (it was a reef): sail in and out at any hour.
                   Harbours take no coin; they fill the lamp, sailcloth and hull once a day and keep one thing from
                   the hold, and only come down on a low-water night (round a new or a full moon).


6. THE PUZZLES, REGION BY REGION
--------------------------------
All of these are read from the world. No markers, no tutorial text, no reward popups. Mara speaks at most once.
"(demo)" means it is in the demo too.

HOME SOUND
  Noon slack (demo)
    Near Tama around noon the water goes glassy, the cat's paws stop and the current streaks fade; the bell swings
    once at twelve. From 11:30 to 12:30 within 260 of Tama. Nothing to do: a breathing space to drift, anchor, fish.
    (No longer tied to the bell's stroke count, so the bell decision is free.)
  Fishers' marks (demo)
    By day you keep seeing the same three fishing boats at anchor in the same places. Sail up to one and look past
    it: two tall things (Tama's lighthouse and something behind it) stand in line. Fish there: bites come sooner
    (x0.7 wait) and the right fish bite.
  The empty mark (demo)
    After three in the afternoon the boats row home. Sail to where one lay, line up the same two things, anchor and
    fish: a morning snapper bites in the afternoon; a golden grouper bites with no wreck in sight.
  Gulls go home (demo)
    At dusk in Home Sound a loose V of gulls crosses ahead of the bow, always flying towards Tama (at dawn away
    from it). Follow them and you are home, no dot needed.
  The Sunday race (demo)
    Be in Home Sound on a Sunday after one. Off Tama four small boats sit beside a committee boat with a big flag.
    At two the flag drops and they make sail. Follow them round the isle they head for and back past the flag boat,
    using gusts and currents. Beat at least one: Tama's board has "The regatta purse" for you (30 to 60 gold).

CROWN
  Pennants come down (demo)
    Take a timed delivery to a Crown harbour. Its tall white signal mast flies one pennant per hour you have left
    and strikes one each hour. None left: you are late.
  Rowing the lane (demo)
    At a Crown harbour with the wind blowing straight out of its lane, furl and row (G) up between the red and green
    buoys. Mara teases you once. It counts as a clean arrival; clean lines raise Crown pay.
  The line already written (full game, once)
    After the Customs Pier, one long timed run (350 or more) on a Crown board shows its next line in the record
    already written black. Make it on time and the black becomes a tick, with a faint struck-out mark left beside it
    for good. Late: the black line stands. No grace on that run.

FREE CAYS
  Turned-round day (demo)
    Visit a Cays harbour by day: the board is thin ("The quay is asleep. Come back after dark."), no passengers,
    residents or gossip. Come back after five in the evening and everything is there. While the main story needs a
    harbour's people there, the Cays stay awake so the story never waits.
  Lamp out to see the shore (demo)
    At night, looking for a harbour, the far lights are faint inside your own lamp's glow. Press L: the near water
    goes black, and after a moment the far windows and lantern strings come up bright and carry twice as far. Steer
    for them, light up at the pier. Mara says so the first time.

REEF
  Teeth under the tide (OFF with the reefs, 2026-11-26)
  Before it goes
    A storm in Reef water brings a wreck up near you. It stays until the next day, and goes once you are away
    (never while you lie within about 60). Salvage it for the Reef's x1.5.
  The stake with no wreck (full game, after the first throw, once)
    In open Reef water you pass a lone stake with a white rag. There is nothing there; E does nothing. When the next
    blow comes in Reef water, sail back: a wreck has come up right beside it. Salvage it before the next day takes it.
    The stake stays. Nobody says who planted it.

LANTERN
  Keep in sight of a light
    At dusk, a dead light 400 to 600 away across open water, about 45 oil in the lamp. Straight across, the lamp
    drains fast and you arrive short of the 30 a relight needs. Hug the coast past lit lighthouses and it burns a
    quarter as fast. Relight for 30 gold and the local chart.
  Every light its rhythm
    At night each Guild lighthouse flashes its own count: one to three flashes, short or long. Sail close to one and
    open the chart: its pattern is inked beside it in dots and dashes. In the dark or fog, count the flashes and
    match the chart to know which light is which. A dead light never flashes.
  The keeper's supper
    At dusk, a lone lighthouse rock in Guild water: a keeper on the gallery swings his lantern twice. Catch a fish
    first, come within about 45 and press E. A basket comes down on a line, takes the fish, goes up, and comes back
    with a jar: 20 oil. With no fish it comes down empty (Mara: once). Once a day per light.

DROWNED (full game only)
  Low-water nights
    Dock at a Drowned harbour on any ordinary day: the pier is awash under a grey film, and nobody comes down. Come
    back on a night round a new or a full moon, when the sea draws back: they fill the lamp, the sailcloth and the hull,
    and keep one thing.
  A fish for the pier
    The Drowned keep the first thing to hand: a fish, then fruit, then your newest treasure (the market row says
    which before you take it). Carry a cheap fish when you dock and your treasure stays aboard.

DEEP
  Against the gulls (full game)
    Far out, deep in the wrongness, at dusk: the gulls fly out to sea, away from land. Remember Tama's gulls: turn
    the bow to where they come from, and land is that way.

The full menu these were picked from (53 items, with the rest still unbuilt) is in
docs/proposals/REGIONAL_CATALOGUE.md, with its tick-box checklist in section 10.


7. DREAD, THE DEEP, AND THE EDGE
--------------------------------
Dread is one number from 0 to 1, set by where you are. Five stages (stage n = the code's stageOf + 1):
  1  0 to 0.2   Home waters: sunny, gulls, shanties.
  2  0.2-0.4    Off: paler, quieter; people repeat themselves.
  3  0.4-0.6    Wrong: green haze, glowing fungus, standing stones, odd names.
  4  0.6-0.8    Eerie: fog, dead palms, lit empty windows, a magenta lighthouse beam. The lost sailors appear (from 0.6).
  5  0.8-1      Cosmic: black sun, an eye in the stars, the picture itself splits and wobbles.
The music thins as dread rises: horror by taking away, never by adding a scare.
The Free Cays' nights stay under 0.45 while the Two Players play.
Linger at full dread and something wakes: you wake up off Tama. It is not an ending.

The lost sailors (stage 4 and up, each once, never named): a man on a wreck waiting for his stop (his ticket is for
two berths); a rowboat with a fishing line cut clean; a man on a black sandbar digging a hole the sand keeps filling.
They answer the three small wrong things near home (the second berth, the hook already in it, the hole already dug).

THE EDGE (rebuilt this round: now played, not watched)
  Once a voyage, the first time dread reaches 0.9 on open water:
   1. The sea ahead runs to a straight lip about 240 off the bow. Past it, out of the mist, stands a hooded figure
      with nothing in the hood.
   2. The sea starts to pull towards the lip: foam dashes race to it, the wind swings round to blow over it, the
      lamp's flame leans towards it, the music thins.
   3. Nothing locks. Helm, sail, oars, lamp and camera stay yours the whole time.
   4. Sail along the lip, angled a little away from it, and you can hold about 130 out (90 close-hauled).
      Closer than about 50 nothing holds.
   5. One way along the lip: a head on its side with one eye open, then sunk arches. The other way: a tower built at
      wrong angles, then a row of pillars that never get smaller.
   6. A second look is never quite the first. Look at a thing, lose sight of it, look again: the hood has turned a
      little towards where you were; the other eye is open; the tower has one more storey; the row one more pillar;
      one arch fewer stands.
   7. At each end of the stretch white fog closes and the water runs back along the lip.
   8. After two and a half to four minutes the pull grows; nobody stays long.
   Two ways out:
    - Over the lip: white mist, then the ship drifts 180 back, sail slack, pointing at Tama.
    - Beat out against the current, past 390 from the lip (under sail about a minute, with oars a little longer):
      the lip fades back into the mist behind you. Nothing marks it.
  Hands off, it is all over in well under a minute. Played attentively, about four to five minutes.
  The game records which way you left, but nothing uses it yet.
  Screenshots: docs/proposals/edge/.


8. THE STORY UNDER THE GAME
---------------------------
What is true (CANON.md), said plainly here and never in the game:
  - The sea is a closed eyelid. Something under it sleeps and dreams; the world is its dream, and not its first one.
    This is the longest it has slept, so this dream stays. Dread is it stirring.
  - The Lady in the hut keeps it asleep with the die. Each low throw is a moment it nearly woke; the world pays for
    it somewhere. She never speaks (she may be seen).
  - Tama is home because it is where the sleeper is kept gentle: the bells ring softly.
  - Others sat at the table before you. Some did not leave: the lost sailors are earlier captains who went mad in the
    deep. To the Lady all sailors are one: "the sailor". At the Table, once you understand "who counts", she writes
    WHO COUNTS? and under it YOU.
  - Mara is something the sleeper made. Her village belonged to an earlier dream and came over into this one; it is
    no longer above water. Only the captain sees and hears her. Her village comes back to her in four pieces
    (a bell, a street to the water, a blue door, a name she never says). She cannot pick up the die.
  - The Two Players (Teo on guitar, Ilse on violin) sailed too but refused to throw. Their music keeps the Free Cays
    asleep. You find them on still nights only.
  - The Pained Raven, the Other Captain's ship, sails empty, untouched by time.
  - Old Perrin, the cartographer, never left port. Letters only.

The seven questions (understood when sources from two or three regions agree; never shown as a number):
  1 Who counts?             Someone has been writing you down since before you arrived.
  2 What is the Eye?        Something is under the sea, and it dreams.
  3 Who sat before you?     You are the latest, not the first.
  4 What does a throw cost? Your numbers land on other people.
  5 Why is Tama home?       Home is where it is kept soft.
  6 What is Mara?           (see above)
  7 Can it end?             You choose what to do with the last throw.

Threads by region (three wake per voyage): Free Cays, the Music out of the Fog and the Two Players; Lantern, the Bell
Below; Crown, the Ferryman's Coin and the Strange High House; Reef, the Other Captain; Drowned, the Colour and the
Hollow Harbour.

New this round, adopted for one puzzle: in the Reef, a wreck belongs to whoever claims it (lore item L-16, behind
the stake with no wreck). The other 41 lore proposals in docs/proposals/LORE_BIBLE.md are still only proposals.


9. ENDINGS (all at the Table by the Water)
------------------------------------------
  The Long Sleep      throw a 20                         still
  Good Dreams         throw 10 to 19                     warm, a little uneasy
  She Rises           throw 1 to 9                       dark; the Eye follows
  The Tune Is Kept    hum the Two Players' tune          the only gentle, happy ending; no throw
  Your Seat           sit in the other chair             dark; needs question 3 fully understood
  The Unseen Throw    go back to the boat                uneasy
The book weighs the last throw: each earlier high throw lifts it, each low one weighs it, at most 3 either way.
After an ending you may keep sailing or end the voyage (credits, then the title).
The ending texts themselves are still drafts waiting for the writer.

11. WHAT STILL NEEDS YOU
------------------------
Play-feel (nothing here could be judged headless): every new timing and distance is a named constant in the code;
  the Edge's pull and pacing; the Sunday race's fairness; the raider's 0.7 s tell; the stick curve; rumble strength.
Decisions:
  - Clue regions: five clues sit in another region's water (the Ribs, the Sunken Street, the Drowned Bell, the Name
    Wall, the Throwers' Wreck). Retag each clue, or move each place?
  - The bell counts: texts say two strokes, twelve and fourteen; the bell rings once.
  - The Edge: may the Eye wake during it (now: no)? Should "over" or "out" ever mean anything?
  - The stake: should its second look ever feed question 3? Should the lighthouse keeper come out on a dead light?
  - The 42 lore proposals and the 34 unbuilt catalogue items: tick, tweak or cut.
  - Economy: one playthrough with ?econ=1 to get real gold-per-hour numbers.
Not built and left for design: the seabed showing at noon; sleeping figures on the Cays quays; a focus ring for pad
  focus outside the main menus; the hurt raider's smoke has its own look.
Writer: the ending texts, Perrin's five letters, the epilogue lines, the thread clue lines, the credits.
