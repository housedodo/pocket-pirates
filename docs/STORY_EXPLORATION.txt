HOIST: THE EXPLORATION STORY (a companion to STORY_MASTER.txt)
================================================================================================
The main story told by going places. Nothing is carried to the hut; the player learns, and what they learn
changes what they can do at the end. This file replaces "Act 3: the Lady's errands" in STORY_MASTER.txt (part D2)
and fills the world atlas (part F) with places that each hold one piece of the mystery.

  Build  cheap = text only; medium = a new hand-made place with a visit text; larger = a new scene, mechanic or ending.
         Each place below says which.

# X1  PRINCIPLES
################################################################################################

X1-1  The player learns; nothing is delivered.  The Lady is never brought a feather, a clapper or a hat. [CUT: Act 3 errands, see X14]
X1-2  Each region answers one question, from its own angle, and only there. To know it, go there.
X1-3  Attention is the key, not an item: the right hour, tide, weather, season of the week, crew trait, or simply coming back.
X1-4  Nothing is told twice and nothing is explained. The truth is the pattern across places, never one sentence.
X1-5  Sources disagree on purpose (see X11). Two people tell a thing differently; a third thing proves neither right.
X1-6  The world is already marking what matters: bells, ledgers, tunes, lamps. The player is late to a story that was going on.
X1-7  Understanding unlocks OPTIONS at the end, not a better number. The die is still the die. (See X13.)
X1-8  The bright game stays bright. Every clue is first a cosy thing: a baker, a tune, a lamp, a market. The second look is the horror.
X1-9  The HUD never says "clue found". The log gets a new line in someone else's handwriting, and that is all.
X1-10 Every place has a dread version (what dread 3-4 changes), so the same place can be revisited far from home.

# X2  THE SEVEN QUESTIONS
################################################################################################
The mystery is seven questions. A player who has answered few of them still gets an ending; one who has answered most gets more
to do at the table. Each is answered by several places, none of which says it plainly.

  Q1  WHO COUNTS?            Someone keeps a count of everything (names, days, numbers).   Where: Crown, the Keepers, the hut.
  Q2  WHAT IS THE EYE?       The sea is a closed eyelid; something under it is asleep.    Where: Lantern Guild, the far sea.
  Q3  WHO SAT BEFORE YOU?    Others have sat at the table; some did not leave.            Where: Reef wrecks, the Kestrel, the Other Captain.
  Q4  WHAT DOES A THROW COST? Low numbers are bad days; the world pays.                  Where: Crown archive, harbour bells, the Hammock Isle.
  Q5  WHY IS TAMA HOME?      Tama is where the sleeper is kept gentle (bells, a pillow).  Where: Tama itself, on a second look.
  Q6  WHAT IS MARA?          A person from a village that is no longer above water.      Where: the Drowned, the sunken street.
  Q7  CAN IT END?            Sleeping, waking, or something else.                        Where: the Free Cays tune, the table by the water.

################################################################################################
# X3  UNDERSTANDING
################################################################################################
Hidden: state.story.know = { q1: 0..3, ... q7: 0..3 }. Never shown as a number or bar. [PLANNED]

  0  nothing        the player has not met the question.
  1  a hint         one source says something odd (a rhyme, a painted sign).
  2  a glimpse      a second source, in a different region, agrees on one detail but not the rest.
  3  a pattern      the player has seen it across places, and the game lets the log say so (X9). Only now do options unlock (X13).

  A clue gives +1 once (never twice from the same place). Only clues from DIFFERENT regions raise a level past 1.
  A place can carry clues for two questions (see the place blocks).
  Where the player sees it: only in the log, as annotations (X9) and in what villagers say (their lines change at level 2 and 3).
  Cheap: a flag per clue. Medium: log annotations and line variants. Larger: the options at the table (X13).

X-C1  THE CUSTOMS PIER   [IDEA, medium]
  where: a Crown harbour in the first ring around Tama (about 500-700 units); dread stage 0-1.
  how it is found: just sailing; the Board there mentions "customs hours".
  from far: a long grey pier with a flag and a small hut at its end.
  arrival: [DRAFT] "An official in a blue coat waves you to the end of the pier and opens a ledger. He writes: THE MERRY GULL. He writes the date. He leaves the line for the master blank. 'It is always blank,' he says. 'It has always been blank.'"
  later visits: [DRAFT] "The ledger is on the same page. Your ship is in it. So are the ships on the lines above you, back to a ship called the Orchard. Nobody has crossed anything out."
  people: the official (C-NEW-01: "Mr. Hale"; voice: dry, kind, tired).
  LN-C1-1 [DRAFT] "We record every ship that comes in."
  LN-C1-2 [DRAFT] "We record the ones that do not come in as well. They are on the next page."
  LN-C1-3 [DRAFT, only after dread 2 or know q1>=2] "Your entry is already on the next page, Captain. I have only to cross it out."
  choices: 1 "Read the page before yours"  2 "Ask to see the next page" (Talk check)  3 "Leave"
  clue: CL-C1-a  Q1 +1: "The harbour writes down ships that have not arrived."
  second look: at night, the pier lamp lights only when you are alone on it; someone has left a second cup on the ledger desk.
  dread version: the ledger is wet. The name above yours is the one you saw in the Name Wall (X-R1).
  links: X-R1 (names), X-L2 (Keepers' ledger), fragment "A page wrapping eggs" (farm).
  must NOT be told: who writes the ledgers.

X-C2  THE ARCHIVE AT THE GOVERNOR'S FORT   [IDEA, medium]
  where: a Crown town with a church and a tidy quay, about 900-1100 units; dread stage 1-2.
  how it is found: a rumour ("the Crown keeps weather back to the first ship") or a letter of introduction from a Customs official.
  from far: a pale stone fort with one lit window at noon.
  arrival: [DRAFT] "A clerk lets you into a long room of shelves. 'Every bad day of the sea, as far back as we have it,' she says. 'Storms, wrecks, sinkings, bad harvests. We never worked out why we keep it.'"
  the clue (needs a Seamanship or Sharp-eyed check, or a crew member with a wish for a harbour): the dates of the worst days all fall on days when the Lady's die would have come up low, in the way the player's own throws have: [DRAFT] "Next to every black page, in a tiny hand, a number. Never above nine."
  LN-C2-1 [DRAFT] "We only record what happens. We have never asked why it happens on the same days."
  LN-C2-2 [DRAFT] "A boy came last year and asked what the numbers were. He did not come back for the answer."
  choices: 1 "Read the black pages"  2 "Look for the page with today's date"  3 "Leave it alone"
    (2 shows: [DRAFT] "Today's page is already half full. The number is blank. Someone is waiting to write it.")
  clue: CL-C2-a  Q4 +1 ("bad days have numbers"); CL-C2-b  Q1 +1.
  second look: the clerk is at a different shelf each visit; once you have thrown at the hut, a page with your first throw's day is tabbed.
  dread version: the numbers on the black pages are written in different hands, and the last few are yours.
  links: X-C1, X-F2 (the Hammock Isle), the hut's ledger.

X-C3  THE BELLS OF TAMA   [IDEA, cheap: the bells already ring at noon; this adds meaning]
  where: Tama itself. Tama keeps the bells (custom).  Always available.
  how it is found: nobody points at it. The player only notices if they listen.
  the clue: the bells ring at noon. On the day after a throw at the hut, they ring exactly as many times as the throw (a 1 is a single small stroke; a 20 is twenty, the last softly).
  [DRAFT] Mara, the first time: "Funny. I counted fourteen. Did we throw a fourteen yesterday?"
  LN-C3-1 [DRAFT, harbourmaster] "The bells ring the day's number. Always have. Nobody remembers who told us to."
  LN-C3-2 [DRAFT, old woman on the pier] "I stopped counting at nine, dear. I start again at ten."
  inscription (existing, [BUILT]): RUNG FOR THE ONE WHO SLEEPS. RING SOFTLY.
  clue: CL-C3-a  Q4 +1 ("the world hears my number"), Q5 +1 ("Tama is where it is kept soft").
  second look: at dread 3+ the bells ring on their own in the night, with no number, and nobody mentions it.
  dread version: the bell has no clapper (as at the Drowned Bell, X5-bell) and rings anyway.
  links: X5-bell, the Drowned (X-D1).

X-F1  THE TWO PLAYERS   [IDEA, medium; the game's first story use of the night guitar and violin]
  where: two small islands about 350-450 units apart, both in Free Cays sectors, dread stage 0-1. One has a sandbar with a fire (the guitarist); the other a thatch hut on a rock (the violinist).
  how it is found: you hear them. At night, in calm water, each island's tune is audible from about 300 units. They never play together; one plays eight bars, stops, and the other answers.
  from far: a thin line of firelight, and later, a second light across the water.
  arrival (the guitarist, Teo) [DRAFT]: "A man with a guitar on his knee, looking out at the dark. 'She answers,' he says. 'Listen.' Across the water, a violin takes the tune where he left it."
  arrival (the violinist, Ilse) [DRAFT]: "A woman on a rock, bow resting. 'He starts it. I finish it. That is how my grandmother taught me. If one of us goes quiet, the other waits.'"
  LN-F1-1 [DRAFT, Teo] "We have never met. Forty years. We just keep the line going."
  LN-F1-2 [DRAFT, Ilse] "What happens if it breaks? Nothing. That is the odd thing. Nothing, every time we have tried. And then the water talks."
  LN-F1-3 [DRAFT, asked why] "It is manners. We play for the one who sleeps."
  choices (at either island, at night): 1 "Sit and listen" (stay one full night: ship moored, no sleep)  2 "Row across and bring one a message"  3 "Play" (only if a crew member has the Lucky trait or a harmonica ... [IDEA])  4 "Leave"
  the clue: CL-F1-a  Q2 +1 ("it sleeps, and the sea is kept quiet by a tune")  and  CL-F1-b  Q7 +1 ("there is a tune that does something").
  second look: if you sit through the night the two tunes, for the first time, play together for a single bar at dawn; both players look up. [DRAFT] "They did not know that could happen."
  dread version: at dread 3 the tune is the same, but the answer comes from the wrong direction. Neither player is surprised.
  links: X13 (playing the tune at the table, requires CL-F1-b), X-F3.
  build: medium. The game already has the two night voices; this gives them a place and a reason.

  links: X-C2 (the archive's bad days), the throws.
