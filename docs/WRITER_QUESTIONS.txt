HOIST: OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE WRITER
Made 2026-11-18. Every question that is still yours to answer, with enough context to answer it properly.
For each: what it is, when the player meets it, what is there today, what limits apply, and what hangs on it.
Write as much as you like under each one. The full draft texts with their IDs are in docs/WRITER_TODO.txt.

Rules every answer must keep (docs/RULES.md):
  - Horror is implied, never explained. A wrong thing has an ordinary reading first.
  - Lines are short, one idea each.
  - Never in player text: lid, eye, sleeper, Lady, "kept soft", "Mara is from".
  - The Lady never speaks. The captain never speaks. Only the captain sees and hears Mara.
  - Only one ending may feel happy: The Tune Is Kept.
  - The name of Mara's village is never written anywhere.

A. THE END OF THE GAME
================================================================================================================

A1. What does the player read on arriving at the Table by the Water?
    When: the last place in the game, about 2400 north-east of Tama, reached only after understanding clues from
      three regions. It is the moment the whole voyage has been heading for.
    What the player sees: a painted picture above a card. Night sand, a table, two chairs, the book, the die,
      a candle, and a dark shape in the far chair. Under it the card text, then the choices the voyage has earned.
    Today (draft): "A table at the water's edge. One chair on this side, one on the other. A die, waiting."
      If Mara has remembered her whole village, she adds quietly: "I do not think we are here to throw."
    The choices, each shown only if earned:
      Throw the die / Hum the tune the two of them play / Let Mara throw (once) / Ask her who counts (once) /
      Walk round and sit in the other chair / Push your chair in and go back to the boat.
    Also yours here:
      - "Let Mara throw" today: her fingers close round the die and it does not move. "Slippery thing," she
        says, and laughs, a little too quickly.
      - "Ask her who counts" today: she writes WHO COUNTS? on a clean line, and under it YOU. Every line above,
        as far back as the book goes, ends with the same word.
    Open: keep the drafts, rewrite them, or change the mood of the arrival (quiet, uncanny, ceremonial...).

A2. What do the six endings say?
    When: after the player chooses at the Table. Each ending is three to five short lines under its own
      painted sky, then a choice: keep sailing, or end the voyage (credits, then the title).
    Today (drafts):
      THE LONG SLEEP (a throw of 20 after the book's weight; first light, only an old woman in the chair)
        "Twenty." / "She closes the book. For the first time she looks up, and it is only an old woman, very
        tired." / "The sea breathes out. It will be a hundred years."
      GOOD DREAMS (10 to 19; blue night, two lights far off)
        "She writes the number and blots it." / "Far across the water two instruments find each other for a
        bar." / "The sea stays blue. Mara is already talking about supper."
      SHE RISES (1 to 9; the sea lit pink from below; then the waking sequence, and you wake off Tama)
        "She writes the number. Then she puts the pen down." / "Under the table, under the sand, under the sea,
        something that was lying still is not."
      THE TUNE IS KEPT (hum the tune; no throw; Teo's guitar and Ilse's violin play)
        "You hum the eight bars. Your voice is not good. It does not matter." / "The die stays where it is. The
        pen stops." / "Far off, a violin answers. It always did need two." / "Nobody throws tonight."
        Mara, afterwards: "I suppose one of us ought to learn the guitar."
      THE UNSEEN THROW (push the chair in; the table far behind, your steps in the sand)
        "You push the chair in and walk back down the sand." / "Nobody calls after you. Behind you a pen starts
        again, on a new line." / "There is a blank in the book where your number would be. It will stay there."
      YOUR SEAT (sit in the other chair; the far chair empty)
        "You walk round the table and sit in the other chair. It is warm." / "The book is in front of you. The
        pen is in your hand. You know every name in it." / "Across the table Mara sits down, slowly. She does
        not ask." / "A die waits on the wood."
    Limits: only The Tune Is Kept may read as happy; Good Dreams is "warm, a little uneasy"; the others stay
      still, dark or uneasy. Nothing may explain what is under the sea.
    Open: rewrite any or all; say for each what feeling the player should leave with.

A3. What is the line left in the Ship's book after each ending?
    When: after an ending, one line appears in the Ship's book "in another hand", as if someone else logged it.
      The player sees it when they keep sailing or load the voyage later.
    Today (drafts):
      long sleep: "Logged: a twenty, at the table by the water. The sea has been quiet since."
      good dreams: "Logged: a middling throw. That night the two of them were heard playing together, for one bar."
      she rises: "Logged: a low throw. The next page is wet through."
      tune kept: "Logged: no throw. Somebody hummed. A violin answered."
      unseen throw: "Logged: a blank line, left blank."
      your seat: "Logged, in our own hand: the other chair is warm."
    Open: whose hand is it (the Lady's, Perrin's, nobody's)? Rewrite as you like.

A4. What does her book say at the very end, if anything?
    Context: every throw at the hut is kept with its day; the Ship's book shows a "Counted" tally. The player
      never reads her book itself. At the Table the book is on the table.
    Open: does the player ever see a page of it (at an ending, at the hut, never)? If yes, what is on it?
      Option: it stays closed forever; the not-knowing is the point.

A5. Does Mara's nature ever come up at the end?
    Truth (canon, never said): the sleeper made her; her village belonged to an earlier dream and came over
      into this one. Over the story her village comes back to her in four pieces (a bell, a street to the
      water, a blue door, the name she never says aloud).
    Today: no ending touches it, except the arrival line if her village has fully returned.
    Open: does any ending hint at it (which one, how lightly), or does it stay unsaid forever?

A6. Which wording of what Mara is stands?
    Context: the canon says two things. Section 2: "the sleeper made her". The table of the seven questions
      gives the hidden answer to "What is Mara?" as "Someone who survived something". They can both be true
      (made, and survived), or one is old wording.
    Open: which is the real answer to question 6, or is it both?

A7. What are the credits?
    Today: "HOIST / A voyage. / Thank you for sailing. / The die is back on the table."
    Open: names and roles (writing, music, playtesters, thanks), and whether the last line stays.

B. THE STORY AFTER THE HUT
================================================================================================================

B1. What do Perrin's five letters say?
    When: after the first throw at the hut (full game only), Perrin sends letters that send the captain into
      each region in turn: Crown, Free Cays, Lantern, Reef, Drowned. A letter is done when the captain has seen
      any clue of that region. Mara reads each one aloud.
    Who Perrin is: the cartographer who never left port. Letters only. Dry, precise, a little smug.
    Limits: a letter never says what to find; it only points at the region and a way of looking.
    Today (drafts):
      Crown: "The Crown writes down every ship that comes in. Ask to see the book. Tell me whose name is at the top."
      Free Cays: "In the Free Cays they sleep by day and play by night. Go at night. Take nothing but your ears."
      Lantern: "The Guild's lamps burn a colour I have no name for. You will tell me I am wrong. Go and look."
      Reef: "The Reef counts its wrecks. Count them yourself. I would like a second opinion."
      Drowned: "The Drowned waters have streets, and streets have doors. Go at low water, and count the doors."
    Also: Mara's hint when stuck (the same for all five today: "Perrin wants us in the ... waters. The chart
      shows whose waters we are in."), and her line when the ship needs better parts to go further.
    Note: the Free Cays harbours now really sleep by day, so that letter is now literally true.
    Open: rewrite any letter and the hints; should Perrin's voice change as the voyage darkens?

B2. What line is written in the log the first time each of the seven threads is met?
    When: the threads are seven side stories, three of which wake on each voyage, each in its own region.
      Meeting one writes a line in the log. These lines count as clues towards the seven questions.
    Limits: a fact in another hand that never concludes. Before the hut is known it may not say she, her,
      the hut, or a number.
    Today (drafts):
      Music out of the Fog (Free Cays): "A tune that never ends. Nobody near it sleeps without it."
      The Bell Below (Lantern): "A bell under the water rings the hours of Tama, not of this place."
      The Ferryman's Coin (Crown): "A coin that finds its way back to the purse it left."
      The Strange High House (Crown): "A house that keeps a book of who stayed. Our name is in it."
      The Other Captain (Reef): "A ship like ours in the wrong colours, lower in the water."
      The Colour (Drowned): "A patch of sea the wrong colour. The fish from it taste of nothing."
      The Hollow Harbour (Drowned): "A harbour that knew we were coming, and had a place set."
    Open: keep or rewrite each.

B3. Does the hut change on later visits?
    Context: the player can throw once a day at the hut. Each low throw adds a candle (that is canon). The hut
      otherwise looks the same every time.
    Ideas, none built: a second chair appears; a page left open with your ship drawn on it; after a natural 1
      the hut is empty on the next visit.
    Open: which of these, something else, or nothing.

C. THINGS ADDED THIS ROUND
================================================================================================================

C1. Should the way the player leaves the Edge ever matter?
    What the Edge is: once a voyage, deep in the dread on open water, the sea runs to a straight lip and falls
      into the dark. Past it stand things far too big: a hooded figure with nothing in the hood, a head on its
      side, a tower at wrong angles, pillars that never get smaller, sunk arches. Nothing explains it.
    What changed: it used to be watched (about 45 seconds, the helm locked). Now it is sailed: the sea pulls
      towards the lip; the player can sail along it, see each thing in turn, and each one is subtly different on
      a second look.
    Two ways out: over the lip (white mist, the ship drifts back pointing home), or beating back out against the
      current (harder; the lip fades into the mist).
    Today: the game remembers which, and nothing uses it.
    Open: should "over" and "out" ever lead anywhere different (a line, a clue, an ending), or stay meaningless?

C2. May the thing that wakes at full dread wake during the Edge?
    Context: if the player lingers at the deepest dread, a waking sequence plays and they wake off Tama (not an
      ending). During the Edge its clock is paused, so it cannot cut the Edge short.
    Open: keep it paused, or let it wake even there?

C3. Should the stake with no wreck count as a clue for "Who sat before you?"
    What it is (Reef waters, full game, once, after the first throw): the player passes a lone stake with a white
      rag in open water. Nothing is there. When the next storm comes, a wreck has risen right beside it. The stake
      stays. Nobody says who planted it.
    Its ordinary reading: Reef salvagers mark wrecks they mean to claim (adopted lore: "a Reef wreck belongs to
      whoever claims it"). The second look: it was marked before it sank.
    Today: it counts for nothing in the clue ledger.
    Open: should seeing the risen wreck add a clue line (towards question 3 or another), and if so, what line?

C4. Does the lighthouse keeper come out when his light is dead?
    What it is (Lantern waters, at dusk): on a lone lighthouse rock a keeper swings his lantern from the gallery.
      Come close and press E: a basket comes down on a line. Put a fish in it and it comes back with a jar of oil.
    Today: if his light is dead that night, he does not appear (the "relight" action takes that spot).
    Open: should a dead light's keeper still be there (in the dark, not waving), or is the absence the point?

C5. Nine new draft lines were written during the build. Keep or rewrite?
    Mara, once each:
      - Rowing up a Crown harbour's buoy lane: "Oars, Captain? In front of the whole town?"
      - First night in the Cays with the lamp out: "Lamp out a moment. There. Lights."
      - The keeper's basket comes down empty: "No fish for the keeper, Captain?"
    Prompts and boards:
      - While the basket comes down: "Calling up to the gallery..."
      - At a keeper's rock: "[E] Lie to under the light"
      - A Free Cays harbour board by day: "The quay is asleep. Come back after dark."
      - Tama's board after the Sunday race: "The regatta purse"
      - A Drowned harbour at high water: "The planks are under water. Nobody comes down."
      - Selling, when only errand goods are aboard: "kept for your errands"

D. THINGS THAT DISAGREE
================================================================================================================

D1. How many strokes does Tama's bell ring?
    Context: Tama is home because the bells there ring softly (canon). Three texts give three counts:
      a harbour text "TWO STROKES, NEVER THREE"; a greeter "Twelve, every day"; and a clue line, "Tama rang
      fourteen at noon." The bell in the game rings one stroke at noon.
    Open: is there one true count (and which), or do the sources disagree on purpose (the game does use
      disagreeing sources as a device elsewhere)? If on purpose, should the bell you hear match any of them?

D2. How many crew are there?
    Context: two lines: "The crew keeps counting to eleven. We only have nine." and elsewhere "Eleven. We are ten."
      Both are at high dread. The captain's crew is otherwise never counted.
    Open: on purpose (the count keeps slipping), or one number?

D3. Five clue places sit in another region's water.
    Context: each clue counts for one region (that is how Perrin's letters and the deep gates are opened). The
      chart decides a place's region by the water it stands in. For five places these disagree:
      - The Ribs: counts as Reef, stands in Lantern water.
      - The Sunken Street: counts as Drowned, stands in Free Cays water (on purpose once: a "distance lie").
      - The Drowned Bell (counts as home), the Name Wall and the Throwers' Wreck (count as Reef): between regions,
        depending on the voyage.
    Effect today: e.g. seeing the Ribs finishes Perrin's Reef letter while the ship is in Lantern water.
    Open: for each, change the clue's region to match the water, or move the place, or keep it as a deliberate lie.

E1. Should "the Eye" ever be named?
    Context: "eye" is banned in player text (RULES 6). Three lines use it at full dread: the place name on the HUD
      becomes "THE EYE"; an island on the chart is named "The Eye"; the waking sequence shows "THE EYE OPENS."
      An older note of yours allowed the word "before the end"; the current rules have no exception.
    Open: allowed at the very bottom of the dread, or never (and then what replaces them)?

E2. What about the stock horror lines?
    Context: at full dread the HUD, the chart and Mara start to lie. Some lines sound like any horror game:
      "TURN BACK", "it is behind you", "you were never here", "Look behind you", "Where you drowned", and Mara's
      "turn back turn back turn back...", "Don't look at the water. It looks back. It looks back. It",
      "I see you, Captain. I see you seeing me." The chart also writes "TURN BACK" in large letters over the ship.
    Lines from the same lists that already sound like this game: "the same water", "HOME?", "Tama?",
      "The stars are not where I left them."
    Open: keep, cut, or rewrite each in the game's own flat, ordinary voice.

E3. How should the text break at full dread?
    Context: today letters on the HUD and in Mara's box are swapped at random for block characters. The rules
      ban symbol glyphs; Mara's own lines already break by being cut off mid-word.
    Open: keep the blocks (the one place the interface itself breaks), or break text only with dropped letters,
      repeated words and lines cut off mid-word?

E4. Should the hut sound less like a shop?
    Context: after a good throw the line ends "(+N gold, and <trinket>)". When you have already thrown today:
      "The die is gone from the table. She is writing. Come back tomorrow." The gold is a real game rule; the
      question is only how it is said.
    Open: keep, or say it another way (or not at all)?

E5. "A natural twenty. Somewhere far below, something is disappointed."
    Context: shown on rolling a natural 20 at the hut. It says there is something below and what it feels,
      which breaks "never explain".
    Open: keep, rewrite, or cut?

E6. Greeters who name the cause after a throw.
    Context: after a low throw, some harbour greeters say things like "Somebody threw a {number} and the whole sea
      went soft". There is also a trial (off by default) where a low throw "lands" on one harbour.
    Open: keep these greeters, cut them, or only keep them if the "landing" trial becomes part of the game?
