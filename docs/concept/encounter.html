Sea encounters, in the game's own style

Redesigned to be small. No separate scene and no new screen: the world stays as it is, your ship slows, and a short exchange opens in the same box villagers already speak in. A rare bit of variety at sea, never part of the main story and never required.

C · The result. The pixel die shows its throw where the portrait was, with the roll in the speaker line. One short reply and at most one small gain, shown with the same toast style as other rewards. A low throw changes something small instead of hurting you: a stranger who stops answering, a worse price at the next harbour. Then the box closes and you sail on.

How rare, and how separate
How oftenAbout one every three or four days of sailing. Never in a harbour, never in fog or a storm, never twice in a row, never in the first hour. StoryNone of them advances the main story or a side story. At most they leave an item for the log or a single, small remembered line. RequiredNever. A player who sails past loses nothing but a little variety. What the die doesDecides how generous the stranger is. High throws give a little extra; low ones change a small thing (a price, a mood). ExamplesA boat adrift with a lit lamp. A bottle with a joke in it. A fisher asking the time. A gull that follows the ship all day. A turtle on a log. ToneMostly cosy and odd. Now and then slightly wrong, so the dread stays something a player may take for a one-off.
