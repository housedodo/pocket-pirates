One-time encounter scenes

At sea a rare sign (a boat adrift, a light where none should be) can open a short scene. Four approaches, one die. Each scene happens once per voyage and the world remembers how you handled it. Nothing here is "win or lose": every outcome moves something.

A · The choice. The scene sits over the sea you were already sailing, in the light of the hour and with the weather you had. Five approaches along the bottom, in the same signpost style as the harbour: Talk, Sail, Look, Offer or just Leave (no roll, a quiet outcome of its own). The scene text is two or three lines and implies. The box on the right is how the world has already noticed you, never an explanation.

B · The outcome. The die result shows with its roll, then a short line of what is said and one small gain. Then two or three plain follow-ups. High throws are generous; low throws are not punished with damage but with something changing: a worse price tomorrow, a stranger who stops talking, a second encounter nearby. Whatever happens is remembered: the same stranger can return at a harbour, and the fishing boat of the nearest village changes with the choice. Scenes are one-shot per voyage so that nobody can farm them.

Each is a dice scene with four approaches. They are rare, appear once per voyage, and are never required.

C · What opens a scene. Five starting points, to show the range: the sea, a ship, the shore and your own crew. They are kept rare (about one a day, never in a harbour) and always optional, so the game stays calm for a player who sails past. Later scenes are tied to the schedule: a boat adrift only on Fridays, the wrong-way ship only on regatta day.
