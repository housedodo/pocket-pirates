Harbour welcome: five layouts

Same harbour, same lines in each: the greeting ("Back again, captain!") and the odd line ("Same tide as yesterday. Exactly the same. Down to the ripple."). In every layout the shipwright's line moves into the Shipwright tab, so the welcome stands alone.

1 · Arrival card. Docking first shows a full-screen card over the darkened harbour: name, greeting, then the one odd line on its own, large. A key press goes ashore and the normal menu opens. Can't be missed, one beat of silence before shopping. Cost: one extra key press each visit (could show only on the first visit of the day).

2 · Someone on the pier talks. The welcome comes from a villager in the same typewriter box Mara uses, with a little portrait, while the menu sits to the side. It types out slowly, so it gets read like dialogue, not skimmed like a label. Familiar from the rest of the game; the villager changes per visit.

3 · A note pinned to the board. The harbour name is a carved wooden sign; the odd line is a paper note nailed to the top of the board, slightly crooked, in dark ink. Feels found, not told. Later the notes can be written in a shakier hand, smudged, or torn as dread rises. The greeting moves into the sign's subtitle or disappears.

4 · Split view, speech bubble in the world. The menu becomes a panel on the right; on the left the camera frames the village, and the line appears as a speech bubble over an actual villager. The village stays visible, so the line belongs to a place and a person. Needs a camera move on docking.

5 · Overheard banner. The menu drops low and compact; the top third of the screen is a banner with the harbour name and the overheard line in large, slightly wrong-coloured letters (pale rose, warming to magenta with dread). No greeting at all: the strange line is the welcome. Quietest of the five, and it never needs an extra key press.
