Quest cues: done, and what is next

The problem: a finished quest and a new one are easy to miss (a small toast, a changed line in the tracker). Four designs, from loudest to quietest, each meant to answer the same two questions the moment something changes: "what did I just finish?" and "what do I do now?". They can be combined; the idle nudge at the end works with any of them.

A · Banner sequence. The loudest. When a quest completes, a big gold "QUEST COMPLETE" banner drops in with the reward and a coin sound; two seconds later it slides up and the "NEW QUEST" card appears under it with the next objective in large type. The tracker flashes gold and shows a red NEW tag, and the wind ring's orange dot pulses with rings. Nobody can miss it, and it fits the game's pixel look. Cost: it interrupts, so it should only fire for main quests, not for every little side goal.

B · The quest scroll. A parchment scroll unrolls on the right: the finished quest gets a green stamp, below it the next quest is written out with a direction. It stays until you press E or sail on for a while, so you can read it at your own pace, and the same text is kept in the journal. Warmer and quieter than A, and it feels like the book of the game. The bottom half is where "what do I do now?" lives, including the direction.

C · The pointer. No pop-up: a big gold arrow and a name with a distance appear on the edge of the screen, pointing toward the next objective for about ten seconds, and the orange dot on the wind ring pulses with rings. A single line at the bottom says what the new quest is. It answers "where do I go?" directly, which is the real problem when you do not know what to do next. Very cheap to build, and the least intrusive; it works best for quests that have a place.

Mara, first mate

Done! 120 gold, Captain. Now spend some of it: the shipwright at Tama will have something for the sails.

D · Mara says it, the tracker sparkles. The mate is already the voice of the game, so she delivers it: a short line that says what was finished and what to do next, with pixel sparkles bursting around the tracker, which grows to show the ticked step and the next one in gold with a NEW tag for ten seconds. Keeps the tone cosy and in the world; the weakness is that it relies on reading a typewriter line, so it works best together with C's arrow.

The idle nudge (works with any of A to D). If nothing has progressed for about a minute, the tracker's current step pulses and a small card at the bottom spells out the next step and its direction, with "Y: ask Mara" for a hint. It disappears the moment you make progress. This is the safety net for the player who did not read the banner or got distracted fishing: no text wall, only a reminder that wakes up when it is needed. It also replaces most of Mara's slow hint timer.
