# Lore connections: proposals (2026-11-19)

Every line here is a **proposal**, not canon. Nothing goes in `docs/CANON.md` until the owner picks it. Built only from what
`CANON.md` already holds; where a proposal touches something section 8 lists as open, it says so. In-game text follows RULES 1-11
(no lid, eye, sleeper, Lady; implied, never explained).

## 1. Structural gaps: what a local, a clerk or a sailor would ask

1. **Who reads the records?** The Crown writes every arrival, the Fort keeps every bad day, the Keepers' Ledger has dates not yet
   come, her book has every throw. Nobody in the world is ever seen *reading* any of them.
2. **Why are the archive's numbers never above nine?** It matches She Rises (1 to 9) exactly, and no clerk has wondered aloud.
3. **What feeds the Guild's oil?** It "comes up by itself" from a fish nobody names, and the dark burns it faster. Nobody asks what
   gives it, or why it comes up only for the Guild.
4. **Who redraws the charts?** "On the old charts" is how the world speaks of before, and places were not always called what they
   are now. Perrin, the man who draws charts, never remarks on it.
5. **Where is R.?** R. threw bottles from the hut and now writes from every water, in any order, with no date.
6. **What took the Drowned Bell's clapper?** The bell hangs open in the lantern with no clapper, and now a clapper comes up on a line
   nearby, too big for any bell anyone knows.
7. **Why does music work in the Cays and nowhere else?** The Crown rings loud and finds Tama too soft; the Cays light a fire "for the
   players" that does nothing.
8. **What does the diver wait for?** Seen once, upright on the water, after the first throw. Nothing asks.
9. **What do the Drowned keep things for?** They take one thing from the hold and it comes back once, out of the deep.

Gaps to leave as gaps on purpose: where the table came from (L-04: no founding story exists) and what Mara is (CANON: a mystery
forever).

## 2. Connective tissue

### A. Every ledger is a copy (the Crown, the Fort, the Keepers, her book)
- **The link:** the world's record keepers may be copying one book without knowing it. The Fort's numbers beside the bad days are her
  throws; the Keepers' future dates are days a captain will sit at the table; the Crown's blank master line is blank because the name
  is written elsewhere first. WHO COUNTS / YOU closes it: every line in every ledger is the same sailor.
- **The unreliable veil:** clerks who disagree. One Crown clerk insists the archive is older than the Crown; another says it was
  started by a governor whose portrait nobody can find. A margin note in the Fort (section 4) says a number cannot be added. A Cays
  harbourmaster shrugs: "In the Cays we do not count, so nothing is wrong with our numbers."
- **Two readings:** the world's people copy her out of a habit old enough to be custom (people making patterns); or the dream writes
  through anyone holding a pen.
- **What stays hidden:** which book is the first; whether anyone reads them; what writes the number back.

### B. The lights look away (the Guild's oil, the hungry dark, the still beams, "blue so the sea does not notice you")
- **The link:** the Guild says it counts lights, not ships (L-19), and keeps them burning blue (L-40). The oil comes from below and
  burns faster where the dark is deeper. One light's beam lies still and points out through the wall. The lights may be there so that
  something below sees light and not ships: a lamp to look at instead.
- **The unreliable veil:** a lamplighter's saying with no reason given ("keep it burning so it has something to watch"); a dead
  light's keeper who stands with his back to the sea and will not turn round (C4, already built); passengers who disagree about
  whether the blue is the oil or the glass.
- **Two readings:** the Guild protects ships by giving the deep something else to watch; or the Guild feeds it, and the oil is what
  it pays back.
- **What stays hidden:** what the fish is; what the still beam points at; whether anything below looks at all.

### C. R. is the sailor before you, or you (the small wrong things, the lost sailors, R.'s letters, the Kestrel, the Marigold)
- **The link:** the two berths, the hook already in the catch and the hole dug first are answered by the three lost sailors in deep
  water. R.'s letters come from every water in any order, with no date. Side seed 6 in the writer's notes ("letters from a captain
  on your route; the handwriting slowly becomes yours") fits R. exactly.
- **The unreliable veil:** R.'s hand drifts letter by letter toward the hand that writes the Merry Gull's own clue lines; two
  harbourmasters disagree, one saying R. came through last week, the other before his grandmother. The cup says MERRY GULL "in a
  hand you do not know".
- **Two readings:** R. is one earlier captain, and every captain ends up writing the same things to the next (insignificance); or R.
  is the captain on an earlier turn of the dream (WHO COUNTS / YOU, the loop).
- **What stays hidden:** R.'s name, R.'s ship, whether R. ever reached the table, and whether R. is you. R. stays an initial (L-36 A).

### D. The sea is sung to sleep (Tama's soft bell, the Two Players, the Cays' fire, the Hammock Isle, the missing clapper)
- **The link:** where it is quiet, things stay as they are: Tama rings softly; the Cays keep a night without raiders while somebody
  plays; on the Hammock Isle people sleep by day and murmur numbers. A bell with no clapper and a clapper too big for any bell:
  someone may once have silenced a bell that rang too hard.
- **The unreliable veil:** the Crown's ringers mock Tama's soft bell as lazy (L-10); the Drowned say bells are for calling names home
  (L-22), not for quiet; the clapper's buyer only says nobody has a bell that size.
- **Two readings:** the sound keeps something asleep; or sound means nothing and people tell themselves stories about the quiet
  they are lucky to have.
- **What stays hidden:** what hears; who took the clapper; why the Two Players' tune works where a bell does not.

## 3. Mythos deepening: three small elements

1. **A Reef superstition:** Reef crews scratch the seventh face off any die they find, "so it cannot come up". The Marigold's die
   with no seven is then either one of theirs, or the reason they started. Feeds Q4 (what a throw costs); fits link C. A customs
   line, once.
2. **A document: Perrin's correction slips.** A sea chest remnant: strips of paper a chartmaker pastes over a coast that has changed.
   Peeling one back shows an older slip underneath, and under that the coast drawn exactly as it is now. The charts change, and the
   corrections keep putting it back. Feeds "the world was not always what it is". Fits links A and C; touches Perrin's letters
   (open, CANON section 8), so the slips carry no words of his.
3. **An environmental detail:** the tide poles in Drowned harbours carry two scales. One is in fathoms. The other has no units, is
   marked in a hand nobody there claims, and its lowest notch sits well above the highest water anyone remembers. Nobody comments.
   Fits link D, and the "keep and are never thanked" of L-24.

## 4. A sample in-world text (link A in action)

A slip of paper in the Governor's Fort, tucked into the archive's first shelf. It could be a remnant in the sea chest, or a second
visit's line.

> Note for the next clerk.
> The black pages are in order. The small numbers beside them are not ours. I have checked every hand in the building. None of them is above nine.
> I tried to add one. I wrote a ten beside a bad day in the spring before last. In the morning it was a one, and the ink was dry.
> Do not try to add one.
> (the name is scratched through)

Readings it leaves open: a clerk who misremembered, a colleague's joke, damp ink that ran, or something that keeps the count.
