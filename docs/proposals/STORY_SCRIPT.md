# HOIST: the script of one voyage (a reading script, not canon)

What this is: one possible voyage, told as a script, from the first morning at Tama to an ending. It is built from what already
exists in the game and the docs. It decides nothing. Where the game has only a draft, the draft is used and marked; where something
is open (`docs/CANON.md` section 8) it is left as a gap and marked.

**Tags**
- `[OBJ-0095]` an existing line, verbatim, with its ID from `docs/STORY_MASTER.txt` (or its clue or table id).
- `[DRAFT ID]` a line that is only a draft in the game (Perrin's letters, the ending texts, the epilogue lines, R.'s letters, the pilots' sayings, the lore proposals). Used as it stands. Never a final.
- `[NEW]` a line written for this script. Few, short.
- `[GAP]` something open that this script does not fill.
- Stage directions are untagged: present tense, short. Silent wrong things appear only as stage directions or as the game's own flat text. Nothing is explained. The captain never speaks. `BOOK:` is a line in the Ship's book, `SCREEN:` a line the game prints.

**Order.** Acts I, II, IV and V run in order. **Act III is the player's choice**: Perrin's five letters can be followed in any order, and a
letter is done when its water has shown anything at all. This script goes Crown, Free Cays, Lantern, Reef, Drowned. The clue places
(the Customs Pier, the Fort, the two players, the Bell, the Ribs, the Street) can also be met in any order, or by accident while sailing
to another. The wrong things near home, the one-head tells and the lost sailors each happen once, whenever the player gets there.

# ACT I: FAIR WINDS
*(Home Sound, Chapters I to III)*

## 1. Tama pier, dawn, fair, light wind from the west

The Merry Gull at the end of the long wooden pier. Gulls. A pale morning. Mara stands on the foredeck, hat on, rope in hand.

MARA: Morning, Captain! I am Mara, your first mate, and this is the Merry Gull. She is small, but she is ours. [OBJ-0002]

The captain takes the helm. The ring on the water goes gold under the bow.

MARA: There she goes. A and D steer her, and the wind does the rest. [OBJ-0003]

A fisher coils a line on the pier as the Gull passes.

FISHER: You are up early, captain. The sea is always politest before breakfast. [GRT-0004]

A woman with a basket stops at the bell post at the pier's end. The rope is worn smooth.

VILLAGER: We ring it gently here. It carries further that way. [CUST-0001]

The Gull sails a hundred units out and comes back. The harbourmaster waves from the pier.

MARA: Nicely done. Now bring her back to Tama's pier and press E. The harbourmaster was waving at us. [OBJ-0008]

HARBOURMASTER: Sign the book, captain. Anywhere is fine. The pages fill up on their own anyway. [GRT-0040]

The captain signs. The harbourmaster holds out a folded letter.

MARA: Old Perrin left something for you with the harbourmaster. [OBJ-0009]

## 2. Hollow Cay Galen, morning, fair

Perrin's riddle is in the orders. The captain reads it and sails to the shore it means.

BOOK: Where the sun is born, the chest lies sleeping. [LORE-0067]

The anchor drops. The captain digs along the sand. Square after square of dark wet sand. On one shore there is a hole already, deep, its edge soft with wind. Mara looks at it, looks at the captain, shrugs.

SCREEN: Somebody dug here first, and stopped a spade short. Lucky us. [MAIN-0282]

The chest is three squares along.

MARA: Ha! Treasure! A captain with gold in the hold is a captain with options. [OBJ-0016]

BOOK: A hole dug first, a spade short of the chest. [CLUE-0020]

## 3. Open water off Tama, noon, dead calm

The sea goes flat. The flags go slack. The ring on the water is empty.

MARA: Not a breath of wind. Press G for the oars, then W to row us a little way. [OBJ-0024]

The captain rows, stops, casts a line. A bite. The needle crosses the green marks. A fish comes up over the rail with a hook already in its mouth, old bronze, the line cut clean. Mara reaches for it, looks at it, drops it in the bucket with the others.

BOOK: A bronze hook already in the catch. The line cut clean. [CLUE-0019]

MARA: Dinner AND exercise. A full day's work for a captain. [OBJ-0026]

A breeze finds the sails.

MARA: And listen: there is the wind again. Sails up, Captain. [OBJ-0027]

At the fish stall on Tama's quay the fishmonger weighs the catch, finds the hook, holds it to the light.

FISHMONGER: "Second one this week," says the fishmonger, and drops the hook in a jar. [MAIN-0264]

## 4. A passage to Velto, afternoon, fair

The Board at Tama offers a passenger: a net-mender with a heavy bag. She talks the whole way.

PASSENGER: You don't say much, Captain. My wife was the same. Forty years and I never won an argument. [PAS-0107]

MARA: The captain doesn't argue. The captain just steers where they meant to go. [PAS-0108]

PASSENGER: You sail alone a great deal, don't you, Captain? I can always tell. [PAS-0115]

MARA: Alone is a strong word. [PAS-0116]

Velto. The passenger steps onto the quay and waves. The fare comes in, double.

SCREEN: (+g, two berths) [harbour.js, no ID]

The harbourmaster of Velto looks along the deck, from the bow to the stern, at the empty bench by the mast.

HARBOURMASTER: "And the other one?" The harbourmaster looks along your deck, then shrugs and stamps the book. [MAIN-0261]

BOOK: The first fare paid for two berths. One stayed empty. [CLUE-0018]

Beside the pier a man hangs nets.

VILLAGER: Velto. Nets, gulls, pie. Mind the second step. [CUST-0002]

The captain charts eight islands over the next days. The fog stands a little further off each time.

MARA: A fine start to a map. Perrin would be proud. Probably. Nobody has seen him smile. [OBJ-0045]

# ACT II: LOOSE TALK
*(Chapters IV to VI: to the hut, the first throw, the diver)*

## 5. Velto tavern, evening, warm

Lamps, dice, a fiddler by the door. The captain hires a hand, who says nothing and sits by the fire.

MARA: Good. A crew is only as good as the company it keeps. [OBJ-0049]

A letter waits at the Board in Tama. The harbourmaster brings it out himself.

MARA: Word from Tama: the harbourmaster is keeping another letter for us. Perrin again, I bet. [OBJ-0052]

The captain reads it in the Ship's book: an island on Perrin's chart, far out, and what it should have on its shore.

BOOK: Perrin wants to know if the island has a red rock on its north shore. Go and look. [OBJ-0137, MAIN-0208]

MARA: He wants us to check something on his chart. His note is in the Ship's book. Far out, Captain. [OBJ-0138]

The island is where the note says. The rock is red. Nobody has charted it.

MARA: He was right. About everything. Captain... nobody has ever charted that island. Nobody. [OBJ-0057]

Perrin sends a second riddle. The captain digs a second shore.

BOOK: Where the day goes to die, something shiny waits. [LORE-0071]

MARA: Two chests! Perrin would be insufferable. [OBJ-0071]

## 6. Tama, noon, clear

The captain is at the pier at noon. The bell rings the hour. It goes on a little too long.

BOOK: Tama rang fourteen at noon. [CLUE-0001]

Villagers look up, look down, go on with the day. Mara waits at the rail. The Gull is two hundred units out on the afternoon tide before she speaks.

MARA: We had a bell at home, Captain. Two strokes, every hour. I had forgotten the bell. [TBL-0020]

She adds nothing.

## 7. Open sea, dusk, mild swell

Bottles drift past the bow in twos and threes.

MARA: There are more bottles in the water out here than there used to be. Somebody has a lot to say. [OBJ-0081]

The captain sails into them one by one.

BOTTLE: We counted the crew this morning. Eleven. We are ten. Please advise. [LORE-0047]

BOTTLE: If you find the black hut, do not sit down. I sat down. - R. [MAIN-0206]

BOTTLE: Third bottle I have thrown. The hut is not where I left it. The hut is exactly where I left it. [MAIN-0207]

MARA: They all mention the same thing. A hut. Who writes about a hut? [OBJ-0082]

*(The hut can be heard of from a pier, a bottle or a passenger, whichever comes first. Here it is a bottle; the next scene is a pier.)*

## 8. Harbour tavern, far from Tama, night

The captain sits at the end of a bar. Mara asks around, loudly, for the captain. Nobody answers her; they turn to the captain.

MARA: Harbours hear everything. Somebody at a tavern will know. [OBJ-0085]

An old fisher by the door, to the captain, as if it were a common question.

FISHER: There is a hut out north-east. Black boards. Somebody always home. We row round it. [MAIN-0346]

The innkeeper pushes a corked bottle across the bar, left there by a sailor.

BOTTLE: The hut is north-east of Tama, past where the water goes dark. Black boards, one window lit. Do not knock twice. - R. [MAIN-0348]

Mara reads it off the chart with the captain.

MARA: Captain... look at our chart. There is a hut on it now. I did not draw it. Did you? [MAIN-0215]

MARA: I would rather not go there. But you are the captain. [OBJ-0087]

The fog wall stands at the edge of the chart. A new gap in it, nearer than before.

MARA: That fog is like a wall, Captain. Whatever is out there can wait for us. [OBJ-0108]

## 9. The black hut, dusk, still

A crooked hut on stilts, black boards, one purple window lit. Bones on poles. A dead tree. A ring of pale stones. The captain rows ashore. Mara stays at the boat.

SCREEN: [E] Knock on the dark hut [MAIN-0246]

Inside: a table too big for the hut. One candle. In the far chair, someone the captain cannot see.

SCREEN: A table too big for the hut, one candle, and a chair on the far side, deep in the dark. Something there breathes, slow and patient. A yellowed twenty-sided die waits on the wood. [HUT-0012]

The captain picks the die up.

SCREEN: The die clatters across the table... [HUT-0015]

The die stops on seven.

SCREEN: 7. She says nothing. You are sure she said your name. [HUT-0019, HUT-0007]

SCREEN: She writes something down. She does not say anything. [HUT-0023]

Back on deck. The water around the Gull has gone the colour of tea.

MARA: Captain... is the water thicker? It looks thicker. [MAIN-0022]

MARA: Nothing happened. Why does that feel like something happened? [MAIN-0023]

She keeps her back to the hut.

MARA: I did not like that, Captain. She never blinked. Not once. [MAIN-0026]

Half a minute out. The sea ahead of the bow is flat. A man stands on it. A diver: a rusted copper helmet, the front glass cracked, one side pane gone, a torn suit, the air hose snapped and trailing. Upright, arms down, not looking. The Gull slides toward him. He stays about ten seconds. Then there is only the sea.

MARA: Captain? You went quiet. [MAIN-0234]

SCREEN: The fog thins. The sea is yours. [MAIN-0217]

A bottle at dawn:

# ACT III: GATHER

## 12. The Free Cays, a still night, new moon

By day the harbours are asleep. The Gull comes in at noon to shuttered stalls and a quay with nobody on it.

VILLAGER: Morning, Captain! We are only just up. [CUST-0016]

VILLAGER: Fish after dark, fruit after dark, sleep after breakfast! [CUST-0015]

The letter, read on deck:

PERRIN: In the Free Cays they sleep by day and play by night. Go after dark. Take nothing but your ears. I wrote the tune down once. The page is blank, and I can still hum it. [DRAFT OBJ-0178]

MARA: Perrin wants us in the Free Cays. Let us tie up at a harbour there and ask around. [DRAFT OBJ-0179]

At the Hammock Isle everyone is asleep in the shade: fishers by their boats, a woman with a basket on her knees, a child with one foot hanging.

SCREEN: "What is today?" a woman asks, kindly, and asks again before you have answered. [PLC-0037]

BOOK: An island where everyone sleeps by day. [CLUE-0007]

At dusk, a fire on the quay.

FIRE-KEEPER: I keep the fire. They keep the tune. Everyone keeps something. [CUST-0019]

FIRE-KEEPER: In the Cays we do not count. So nothing is wrong with our numbers. [CUST-0070]

Night. No wind, no storm. The sea is black glass. The Gull drifts between two islands.

MARA: Listen. They are at it again. [CUST-0059]

From a sandbar with a cold fire: a guitar, eight bars, and a stop. Across the water a violin takes the tune where the guitar left it. The captain rows to the sandbar.

TEO: "She answers. We have never met. Forty years. It is manners." [PLC-0026]

TEO: Never sat at her table. Never will. We play instead. [PLC-0067]

TEO: Who began it? The violin did, long before me. I only answer. [PLC-0070]

The captain rows on to a rock with a stool on it.

ILSE: He starts it. I finish it. If one of us goes quiet, the other waits. [PLC-0030]

ILSE: Who began it? The guitar did. I only answer it. [PLC-0071]

BOOK: Two players, forty years, never met. [CLUE-0008]

Mara has gone very quiet and, a moment later, is humming. She stops when the captain looks.

MARA: If I start humming, tell me. I do not always notice. [OBJ-0150]

The captain learns the tune. The dark stays quiet while they play.

OLD SALT: In the Cays they say the dark keeps quiet while somebody plays. [MAIN-0310]

BOOK: A table laid for two. One cup is ours. [CLUE-0009]

The Gull has been in two regions now. In the morning, two hundred units out, Mara puts the next piece down quietly.

MARA: Our street ran down to the water. Down, not along. Odd thing to remember. [TBL-0021]

A bottle in the shallows:

BOTTLE: In the Cays nobody counts anything, not fish, not days. I counted the boats for them anyway. Somebody had already written the number down. - R [DRAFT CL-RL-free]

# ACT IV: COMPARE
*(the deep: the lost sailors, the Edge)*

## 16. The Slack, a grey afternoon, rising swell

The wall stands at the end of the chart. Mara looks at the shipwrights' sign and at the sky.

MARA: The shipwrights say a stronger hull or better sails go further than this, and on a falling tide. Past that, nobody sells charts. [OBJ-0168]

At a harbour on the edge of it, an old salt, to nobody in particular:

## 18. The Edge, night, open water; once

The first time the Gull reaches the deepest dread on open water, the sea ahead runs to a straight lip and falls in one smooth curve into the dark. The helm answers. A current draws at the hull, weak far out, too strong close in. The air goes over the lip too.

The captain sails along it. Things far too big come up out of the dark, one at a time: a hooded figure with nothing in the hood; a great head lying on its side, one half of its face open; a tower built at the wrong angles; pillars that never get smaller; sunk arches. A second look at any of them is never quite the first.

MARA: Captain... it is so big. Why is it so big? [NEW]

*(The line is Mara's one remark. No line of hers names the Edge or what stands past it.)*

*(Two ways out. The sample takes the second.)*
