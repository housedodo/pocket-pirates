# Research: Port Royale and similar sea/trade games, for HOIST

Method note: WebSearch returned only summaries. WebFetch failed on every page (gamingtrend.com, tvtropes.org, uwo-hq.blogspot.com did not resolve in this session's network). Items marked [src] come from search results; items marked [mem] are from my own knowledge and are NOT verified by a fetched page. Treat [mem] as leads to confirm.

## Port Royale (1-4)
- PR3: 60 Caribbean towns, four colonial powers; careers from merchant to pirate [src: https://store.steampowered.com/app/12470]. Convoys of up to 3 fighting ships, manual or automated; storehouse merchants automate buy/sell and hold back raw materials [src: Steam/user review]. Pirates and military fleets threaten convoys, so escorts are needed [src: Steam].
- PR4: each town produces certain goods; big money comes from carrying essentials from producer to a town that needs them. Empty "bar" for a good = town needs it and pays more. After food and shelter are met, citizens look for luxuries [src: https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/a-few-barnacles-on-the-hull-port-royale-4-review/ , https://primagames.com/gaming/port-royale-4-review]. Naval combat went from real-time (PR3) to turn-based (PR4) [src: same].
- PR2: supplying a town with what it needs boosts its economy and draws other traders to set up there [src: https://mobygames.com/game/16226/port-royale-2/]. Older games were seen as deeper in town development.
- [mem] Towns visibly grow (more and larger buildings by population tier); governors' favour gates trade, letters of marque and fleet bases; town-conquest and nation-war layer; events like fire/plague/pirate blockade.
- Criticism [src]: very slow first hours (Midlife Gamer 6/10); no real town defence, a small hostile fleet can take a town; story thin; PR4 reviewers called it "rudderless" (Prima) and "a few barnacles" (Gaming Trend). Praise: the profit loop and the sense of a living Caribbean map.

## Patrician III / IV
- IV: trade routes, political turmoil, disease, weather, piracy, greedy royalty [src: https://store.steampowered.com/app/57620]. A patch made pirate activity a three-level option [src: Softpedia changelog]. Cog ship type exists.
- Reception: Steam Mixed; PC Gamer UK "More yawn than yarr"; users called IV a smaller, simpler Patrician III; GameSpot: poor early purchases can dig an inescapable hole; GameWatcher: a little too easy [src: https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/patrician-iv-review , https://www.gamespot.com/patrician-iv/user-reviews/2200-390473/].
- [mem] Rank/title ladder with council seats as the progression; convoys of ships as automated trade routes; plague and fire events; hanse-wide reputation.

## Uncharted Waters (New Horizons)
- Discoveries are reported to a port's collector (the hornbill example) [src: https://retroachievements.org/game/1293]. Kings react to your highest fame category and hand out matching missions [src: TV Tropes, secondary]. Money comes from trade, guild jobs, gambling, ship battles [src: koei.fandom.com/wiki/Uncharted_Waters_(series)].
- [mem] Fame is split into three tracks (trade, adventure, battle), each unlocking different titles and quests. Discoveries are paid in money plus fame, only once, and only when reported at the right port. Supplies (food, water, crew morale, scurvy) limit voyage length. Each port has a distinct look and culture-specific goods. Many ship classes with different cargo, speed and cannon slots.
- Uncharted Waters Origin (later game): contracts let you file exclusive discovery reports for adventure fame [src: gameplay.tips guide].

## Anno 1404 / 1800 sea and trade layers
- Trade is central; trade routes supply exotic goods to needy, growing population tiers; a pyramid of classes needs balancing [src: https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/anno-1800-review/]. Upgrading tiers too fast starves the lower tier of workers [src: PC Invasion].
- Criticism: trade route UI fussy and error-prone; value of goods not shown; hard with a dozen routes; ships slow; poorly equipped expeditions get recalled [src: Destructoid, PC Invasion, GameWatcher via search].
- [mem] Needs visible as icons above houses and in street life; ports busy with moving cargo carts and ships; city visibly changes with tier.

## Sunless Sea
- Terror rises in dangerous/dark waters far out; high terror leads to mutiny or crew panic; running out of food/fuel is the main pressure; shore leave and finishing stories lower terror; eating the crew is an option [src: https://pcgamer.com/sunless-sea-alpha-review , https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-games-make-me-confront-the-terror-of-open-spaces , https://pcgamer.com/great-moments-in-pc-gaming-nautical-misadventures-in-sunless-sea].
- Terror comes from darkness and distance; lit areas near ports calm it [mem: lanterns and lit ports reduce it]. Exact formula not found.
- Praise: dread, writing, the lonely sea [src: Vice]. [mem] Criticism: grind, harsh early game, permadeath.

## Candidate ideas for HOIST
1. Town supply shows in the streets: bare market stalls, queues, fewer lit windows when a good is short; full stalls when well supplied. (PR4 empty bar; Anno icons.) New, and HUD-wordless.
2. Town growth from your trade: a harbour you keep supplied slowly gains a building, another stall, more boats; a neglected one loses them. (PR2.) Fits cosy loop; later the drowned towns invert it.
3. Needs ladder: food first, then drink/cloth, then luxuries; price premium for the unmet need. Partly covered by price drift; needs layer is new.
4. Discovery reports paid at a harbour: bring a new island, creature log entry or wreck chart to a port's board for coin and standing. (Uncharted Waters.) Partly covered (creature log, treasure maps); the "report" payout is new.
5. Split fame tracks (trade, exploring, fighting) that change which commissions harbours offer. (UW.) Reputation exists; split fame is new.
6. Different regions hand out different jobs by your top track. (UW kings.)
7. Dread as distance from lit harbours: night nerves and mood already exist; tie their rate to distance from a lantern or lighthouse. (Sunless Sea.) Largely covered.
8. Shore leave / tavern rest lowers crew fear. Covered (tavern rest, crew mood).
9. Fuel-like pressure: lantern oil as a stock. Partly covered (lantern, stores).
10. Strange food option that costs sanity; offered on foreign shores. (Sunless Sea.) New, implied horror.
11. Convoy/escort: hire or take a small friendly sloop along for a fee, as protection from raiders. (PR3.) Raiders covered; escort new.
12. Convoy automation avoided: keep trade manual (Anno and Patrician complaints on route UI). Principle; HOIST already manual.
13. Events per harbour: fire, fever, blockade, a feast; seen as a smoke plume, yellow flag, ships queued. (PR/Patrician.) New; wordless via visuals.
14. Pirates as a tunable threat level near trade lanes. (Patrician IV three levels.) Raiders covered; a difficulty-free setting is new.
15. Governors with favour: a letter of marque or pass that unlocks a region's harbours and changes how its raiders treat you. (PR.) Reputation covered; marque is new.
16. Ship classes with distinct hold, speed, gun slots. Partly covered by upgrades; new hull classes optional.
17. Port personality: each harbour has its own silhouette, palette, flags, and music motif. (UW.) Partly covered.
18. Avoid slow-start criticism: give first-hour profit quickly and keep the loop short. (PR3 reviews.) Design rule.
19. No hidden state: show goods value at a glance (Anno complaint); HOIST board/market already shows prices.
20. Mistakes should be recoverable (Patrician "inescapable hole"): a cheap lifeline job when broke. Check if HOIST has one.
21. Expedition recall: poorly stocked voyage forces a turn back (Anno). Covered by stores.
22. Rare finds paid once, then become known to rivals. (UW.) New, small.
23. Dark inversion: the further out, towns' needs become odd (they want things you should not sell), trade turns wrong. (Combines PR needs with Sunless dread.) New, implied.
